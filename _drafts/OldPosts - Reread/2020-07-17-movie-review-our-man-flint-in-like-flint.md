**-OR-One of America's answers to James Bond was pretty goofy.

Note: In an effort to watch movies we've always 'been meaning to watch'; my wife and I compiled a list of 20 movies (10 each from each of us) and we have Google choose a random number to decide the movie.  Sometimes good, a lot of times bad; we've made pretty good progress so far.

Every decade has a defining icon that sums up the entertainment of the mainstream from that time.  For the 60's, it was 'The Dashing Spy' archetype lead by James Bond.  The super spy who combined stealth, charisma and courage into a sexy package that woman couldn't resist and men aspired to be.  But James Bond was British and America, always looking to jump on a band wagon, made various attempts to make our own version of the quintessential spy.  As far as the movies I have seen, the American spy movie tends to go down one of two paths, a procedural drama following the intricate layers of politics surrounding an event or just plain campy parody.

The Flint movies are, of course, parodies of the James Bond series but have a certain charm about them that make them stand out from other parodies or deconstructions of the spy genre.  Examples of the parody spy are Secret Agent Super Dragon, Operation Kid Brother and Leonard Part 6 (The frist 2 are... 'rough' movies but excellent MST3K episodes; The last one is considered one of the worst movies of all time).  These movies typically star males who are too-goofy-to-be-dashing, yet covered in women.

Story-wise, Our Man Flint is all over the place.  The main story begins with 3 scientists, operating as an organization named Galaxy, demonstrating they can control weather using tremors; Using this power, they demand the world governments surrender to them.  A collection of various intelligence offices from all over the world known as ZOWIE (Zonal Organization for World Intelligence and Espionage) collectively agree that the only hope for the world is the dashing, but highly undisciplined secret agent, Derrick Flint.  Then we find out about a drug operation (That goes no where), Flint finds a female Galaxy operative (Who is quickly seduced by Flint with very little effort, but she tricks him to be trapped in a room), locates the secret Galaxy Island (Which is found marked multiple times on map just lying around in the room Flint is trapped in), finds out about galaxy's ability to brainwash women into sexy slaves (Which Flint can quickly reverse just talking to them, but doesn't go anywhere). Finally, Flint confronts the scientists behind Galaxy's weather control system.  They quickly surrender as they didn't want anyone to get hurt they just wanted to bring peace to the world (Despite having the whole brain washing sex island thing), only to be promptly killed by the evil 4th guy in the group.  Next thing you know, Flint is running around fighting this guy while also flipping random switches and turning all kinds of dials to blow up the island.

What the story lacks in coherence, it makes up for with campy dialogue and some unintentionally funny scenes.  Is the story all over the place?  Oh yeah, but it moves quick and doesn't over stay it's welcome.  My editor, enjoying it so much, insisted we also watch the sequel "In Like Flint".  

This is a big deal.  For context, she doesn't like most things. We're married and she just barely likes me.

The sequel, "In Like Flint" is interesting in that it feels like they were going for a pro-feminist message.  But along the way they end up scraping it (The message gets under cut when the president tell the female antagonists to "Leave this to the men" while walking away and laughing. They look, knowing, at the camera as if to say "See you in the sequel, you chauvinist pig").  The movie starts with the launching of a new 'space platform' (Because, it's not much of a late 60's spy story unless something goes into space).  Celebrating the launch, the president invites the head of ZOWIE for a round of golf.  He is promptly kidnapped by the female led Fabulous Face company (FF) and replaced by an actor.  The head of ZOWIE reaches out to Flint due to a discrepancy on the golf course and suspects something bigger is happening in the background.  The scheme of FF involves taking over the space platform to smash the patriarchy and establish a matriarchy.  To stop them, Flint goes on a globe trotting adventure to find the clues, save the president, the space platform, the world, and his 3 girlfriends (Did I mention Flint has 3 girlfriends?  This isn't new, In the first movie he had 4 but mentioned that they were getting out of hand and opted for only 3 girlfriends this time).  Along the way, Flint must survive attacks from the Russians, a Ballet Troupe, his own double-crossing government, and the female assassins of the FF company using his wits, his gadgets, and his ability to communicate with dolphins.

Sounds like a wild ride, right?  Because it is!  My editor and I really enjoyed these movies.  Maybe for the wrong reasons, maybe for the right reasons.  Of course, we would have loved to see the final installment of the Flint trilogy where Flint has the stop the secret Russian / Chinese moon base from firing the communism ray at the United States using Flint's personal high speed rocket ship built from old classic cars he had laying around and the knowledge he acquired by studying robot quantum hydro-rhythms for fun.  But sadly, it wasn't a wacky enough script.

A commonly used character type is a "Mary Sue", described as who is so competent or perfect that they appear absurd.  The male version is typically named a "Gary Stu" but I think I'm going to name these types of characters Flint.  Make no mistake, Flint is the parody of the catch-all super spy.  He has a rotating cast of no less then 3 girlfriends who, despite being highly skilled in various disciplines, always require saving.  He is skilled in a many different fields such as disguising, martial arts, extreme survival, chemistry, gadget creation, dog training, ballet dancing and dolphin communications.  Flint is written with just enough batman-esque "I have a counter for everything because I've thought of everything" sensibility to be entertaining but does sometimes go over board.  

Who would I recommend this movie for...

If you like niche campy movies - Most def.  As for "all over the place" these plots end up being, I highly recommend these.  I'm very much an "MST3K" type and these are great movies to riff on.  They're corny and campy and sexist and wacky all while trying to be serious.  You can tell the film makers wanted to make the first movie with a serious but not too serious tone.  But, by the sequel you can tell they were setting up camp in the 'camp' camp national camp grounds.

Movie Riffers - If you like movies made with earnest, but not a lot of thought.  Yeah, I think you would dig this.
