## -OR- "If only you knew the power of the dark side..." -Darth Vader, Empire Strikes Back

Most of my day, and foreseeable future, consists of staring at a computer screen.  Whether it's work, hobby programming or... um... We'll talk about it when you're older (I play a couple of first person shooters that might not be appropriate for the younger audience).  Because of all my screen time, I try whatever I can to ensure I'm not cooking my retinas or causing long term damage to myself.

To counter / stave off this damage, I've been turning on dark mode if the option is available.  Dark mode inverts the websites usual white background / black text theme to make viewing the content easier.  Look at YouTube before and after:

<pictures of dark read before and after>

The usual theme is fine if your eyes have adjusted to daylight.  But, If you're an early bird or a night owl, being blasted in the face with all that light can force your retinas to unnaturally adjust and / or tricks your brain into thinking it's day time; messing with your sleep schedule.  This is a big reason why I wanted to make my blog dark colored background and gray-ish text.

But many websites don't have the option of a dark mode...

So, Thank goodness for plugins! Specifically, [https://github.com/darkreader/darkreader/commits?author=alexanderby](Dark Reader by a user named alexanderby). An excellent extension that handles the task of inverting the standard white background and black text theme on web pages.

I rate Dark Reader an "Almost fire and forget". The 'almost' is because you may run into the occasional website that gets rendered oddly. To fix this, Dark Reader has a couple of tricks up it's sleeve ranging from easy (Other engines to generate website themes, see above) to a bit more technical (Ability to identify elements and change them manually).

Dark Reader is available for Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge (I mean the new version of Edge, Don't worry if you don't know which one you have, Windows 10 will let you freaking know!)

TL;DR
>Dark reader inverts website colors to make them easier to easier.
>It's robust enough to invert most websites correctly.  
>The occasional page may be rendered incorrectly but dark reader has a couple of corrective measures ranging from easy to pretty technical to keep you covered.
